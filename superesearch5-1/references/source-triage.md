# Source triage and records

Follow the [temporal policy](source-temporality.md) for claim-specific time compatibility. Publication, evidence period, effective validity, knowledge vintage and retrieval are distinct; recent access is not proof of currentness.

Start from one decision question and a missing field, join or acquisition route. Check the existing register, observations and cache before searching. Triage a lead with its locator, holder, potential use, evidence, access status and next action before deep inspection. Public, paid, permissioned, physical, human and unknown-access sources are eligible; access difficulty does not establish quality.

Use a bounded preview to examine headers, sample rows/pages, definitions, units, dates and identifiers. After a failed route, try one materially different route, then record the blocker and move on unless the task authorizes deeper acquisition. Inspect further when a specific next check could change the decision, close a material gap or establish an acquisition route.

Keep observations, inference and unknowns distinct. Register retained sources once with stable local IDs and preserve rejected/deferred leads and reasons in the search log. Record each finding with its question, observation, scope, limitations, evidence locator, acquisition date and next action. Assessment changes must retain earlier judgments and their evidence. Rebuild derived views rather than duplicating authoritative records.

Use host-mandated probe, observation, lifecycle and validation utilities where available. Those project integration tools are external inputs; standalone runs keep equivalent provenance in their working records. Before a discovery round, record its question and baseline; after showing its result, close required source, intervention, yield and lifecycle records. Count distinct supported additions, not duplicate leads, cache hits, failed routes or workflow documentation, as new evidence.
