# Research method

Bundled guidance for the solo researcher on choosing, trusting and reading sources. Part of this skill's version, not a runtime dependency on another skill.

## Sufficiency follows the decision

Infer the required standard from the intended decision, its stakes and the stage the user is at. Separate explicit requirements from provisional assumptions. If a missing parameter does not block useful work, state the assumption and proceed. Do not demand certification where screening was asked for, and do not treat circumstantial evidence as a validated outcome. Do not import constraints from other projects.

## Source hierarchy

For each obligation in the answer map, prefer in this order:

1. **The primary document.** The filing, paper, dataset, standard, official report or engineering post that originated the figure or finding. It supplies the exact value, definition, period and qualifications.
2. **Canonical references of the field.** Established frameworks, thresholds, seminal studies and standards that experts cite by default. These anchor a recommendation and are expected in a complete answer even when product-specific evidence exists.
3. **Specific empirical evidence.** Studies, deployments and measurements that match the question's scenarios. Record design, sample, population, period and the exact measure; a result for one scenario is not a result for another.
4. **Commentary and vendor summaries.** Leads to one of the above; cite them only when the original is unavailable, and say so.

Seek complementary routes when the direct answer is missing: components that can be joined or calculated, comparable cases, the underlying mechanism, or guidance from an authority. Name the weakest join or transfer assumption. Explore enough breadth that the first convenient source does not become the answer, then concentrate on the checks that could change a required result.

## Qualify each consequential use

- **What it says.** Read the whole relevant section: tables, footnotes, definitions, adjacent exceptions and later corrections. Extract the value with its unit, period, entity, definition and exact locator.
- **Trust.** Relevant authority and expertise, accountable authorship, original method or evidence, incentives and stated limitations. An announcement is not delivery; a preview is not a result.
- **Fit.** Publication and evidence dates, effective validity, population, geography, units and definitions versus the question's. Fresh retrieval does not make old evidence current; preserve unknown dates.
- **Independence.** Repeated copies of one observation do not corroborate each other. Note when several sources trace to the same underlying measurement.
- **Access.** Record the route that worked and the ones that failed. A blocked fetch is an access observation, not evidence about the source's value or the fact's absence.

## Definitions before arithmetic

For every requested calculation, write the definition, population, period and denominator before computing. When the question's wording admits more than one defensible definition, compute each from exact inputs and report them labeled; the answer explains which best matches the question. Never substitute a nearby total, subset, rate or proxy silently. Show calculation inputs with their source labels so the reader can reproduce the result.

## Records

Preserve originals or faithful captures with locators. Record each observation once in the applicable note; do not create a second authoritative catalogue. Keep new, deferred and rejected leads distinct from used evidence. Do not invent probabilities, counts or scores to manufacture sufficiency.
