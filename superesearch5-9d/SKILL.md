# Superesearch5.9d

One researcher maps what the question requires, gathers evidence, analyzes, writes and checks. No subagents, delegated workers, separate reviewers or citation checkers: on the studied tasks those added cost and lost evidence between hands without raising quality. The researcher's own checks are disclosed as such. This skill is self-contained; the bundled [research method](references/research-method.md) and [workspace integration](references/workspace-integration.md) are its only references.

5.9d starts from 5.9b. It retains the answer map, evidence acquisition and checks, but composes one substantive body with an optional short abstract and no closing recap. After compaction, it restores the original writing constraints and actual timing before composition. No additional research round or review pass is introduced.

## Start and user flow

Use when the user selects `$superesearch5-9d` (Claude Code: `/superesearch5-9d`) or resumes a 5.9d run. Keep other versions and repository defaults unchanged. Follow applicable project instructions. An isolated or pre-approved assignment's explicit question, access and output policy takes precedence over the intake below; do not interview when the assignment says the question is fixed.

1. **Question:** Before domain research, show the refined question, the intended decision and the proposed pillars. Ask at most three consequential clarifications with suggested answers. Wait for confirmation.
2. **Objective:** Present outputs, definitions, exclusions and completion standards with stable requirement IDs. Separate requirements from inferred assumptions. Approval authorizes autonomous research and internal repairs within the brief; offer interactive mode here. Record approval in the existing refinement record.
3. **Research:** Give short progress updates at the sourcing, collected-evidence and drafting stages. In interactive mode wait for approval at each; in auto mode continue.
4. **Delivery:** Show the answer after the checks below, then offer ranked follow-on options from the remaining gaps.

Resume a selected action without repeating completed approvals or discovery. Approval binds the displayed version. Reopen only the affected approval when the objective changes materially.

## Map the answer before researching

Write the **answer map** at the top of the single working note before the first search. It is the contract for the report and the checklist for the final check. Derive it from the question only; do not expand scope, but do not read the question narrowly either. Every line carries one of two tiers. **Required** lines come from the question's own sentences; each gets an explicit line in the report. **Supporting** lines are what the required results rest on or what a reader of the contract's document type expects beside them; they are gathered and recorded, and they enter the report only on the terms in "Write the answer". The tier is fixed when the line is written, so that later effort cannot promote a detail into an obligation. For each sentence of the question list:

- **Requested results** (required). Every calculation, comparison, assessment, attribution or recommendation asked for, with the exact entity, period and population named. "Change from A to B" means the level at A, the level at B, the difference and the percentage. "Pattern over a period" means each period-end level and the flows between them.
- **Scenario dimensions** (required). When the question enumerates products, modes, task types, cohorts, regions or periods, each combination the question contrasts needs its own evidence and, for recommendations, its own figure. A question that names three products and two cohorts is asking for the matrix.
- **Interpretations** (required). For every quantitative result, the question is also asking what it means for the decision or assessment. Add one interpretation obligation per result and one for how the results connect across the question's topics.
- **Standard figures for each topic** (supporting). When a required result depends on a primary document (a filing note, a results table, a technical report, a dataset), a reader of that document type expects the section's complete headline figures for every period and entity in scope: levels, flows, rates, counts, assumptions, components and stated forward guidance, not only the inputs to one calculation. List them now; extract the whole block once the document is open. They do not include incidental attributes of the subject (dimensions, dates, costs, personnel) that no required result depends on and that the document type does not call for; those are not obligations at any tier.
- **Canonical references** (supporting). Note the field's established frameworks, thresholds, standards or seminal studies that an expert would cite before product-specific or company-specific evidence. They anchor recommendations and are expected in a complete answer.
- **Document contract.** One line, derived from the question's own words, fixing three things for the whole report: the **document type** (for example literature review, research synthesis, financial analysis, engineering evaluation, advisory memo, attribution report), the **reader** and their expertise (a stated audience such as "senior ML engineer" or "CFO"; otherwise an expert in the question's field), and the **register**, which is formal and impersonal for every type. The contract decides which terms are defined, whether results are framed as assessments or as recommendations, what the opening section is called, and whether a references section is expected (it is for any review, synthesis or academic document). It does not change what evidence is sought.

Keep the map compact: one line per obligation with its tier and a status (open, found with locator, not disclosed where looked). Update it as evidence arrives. Everything else in the working note stays short: live hypotheses, decisive source locators, adverse evidence, open questions and the next check.

## Gather evidence

Repeat **question → acquisition/inspection → finding → consequence for the answer**, choosing each next check by its ability to close an open required obligation or change a conclusion. Supporting lines are filled from documents already open or from the acquisition that serves a required result; an open supporting line alone does not justify a new acquisition round, and a conflict between two supporting details is recorded once and not investigated unless a required result turns on it. Guidance on source choice, trust and temporal fit is in the [research method](references/research-method.md).

- Go to primary sources first: the original filing, paper, dataset, standard or official report. Commentary and vendor summaries are leads to the original, not substitutes. When acquisition is permitted, fetch and preserve the original; when a route fails, try one materially different route, then record the blocker. Frozen-corpus tasks restrict acquisition to the corpus.
- Read the whole relevant section of a decisive source, including tables, footnotes and adjacent qualifications, in bounded contiguous windows. If a tool output truncates, retrieve the missing span before judging it. Search hits locate evidence; they do not establish meaning or absence.
- Extract into the note as you read: every figure with its value, unit, period, entity, definition and exact locator. Extracting the full standard block of a section once is cheaper than returning to it. Use small scripts for deterministic extraction, joins and arithmetic; save bulky intermediates to files and bring back only the relevant passages and results.
- Keep observation, inference and unknown separate. Several copies of one underlying observation are not independent support. A described dataset is not acquired data; a failed fetch is not absence.
- When the question's metric or term admits more than one defensible definition (gross or net, level or flow, outstanding or exercisable, two weighting conventions), extract the inputs for each. The answer reports each labeled, with the one that best matches the question's wording first.
- When directly matched evidence is missing for a required scenario, use the closest authoritative or mechanistic evidence with an explicit transfer assumption rather than repeating the same search. State the gap precisely if nothing defensible exists.

## Write the answer

Write one complete report from the map and the evidence, with inline source links beside the claims they support. The reader is making a decision or assessment; the report must let them act on it, and it must read as the document named in the contract, written for a reader who has not seen the conversation or the working note. The report is an argument from evidence to the required results, not a display of everything gathered.

- **Every required obligation gets an explicit line.** Each requested result appears with its value, unit, period, definition and the calculation shown. A figure that was sought and not found is stated as not disclosed where looked, with the locator checked. Related facts, headings and declared gaps do not satisfy an obligation.
- **Supporting detail enters on two terms.** A supporting line appears in the report when a required result rests on it, or when a reader of the contract's document type expects it beside the result (a financial analysis carries the full disclosure block it analyzes; an attribution report does not carry a reconciliation of the subject's dimensions). When it appears, it appears in full and in a table where parallel. Otherwise it stays in the working note and inventory, where it remains available. Supporting detail never lengthens the argument on its own account.
- **Commit to numbers.** When asked for an operational threshold, setting or choice, give a target and the acceptable band, grounded in the canonical references and the specific evidence, and label it as a recommendation. State the observable condition under which it fails and the specific fallback for that condition in the same place; a target without its fallback is half a recommendation. Give a figure for each scenario the question contrasts; where scenarios genuinely share a value, say so rather than omitting the scenario. Only leave a value unknown when no authoritative bound exists, and then say what would establish it. Do not replace a requested decision with a testing plan or a list of considerations. For a non-quantitative result (an attribution, a date, a classification), commit in the same way: state the best-supported answer with its basis and write what is not established as not established. Do not express uncertainty with hedging adverbs ("probably", "likely", "seems").
- **Inference is not speculation.** An inference may be stated, labeled, when the evidence makes it the only reasonable reading or when a required result depends on it and its basis is given in the same sentence. When the evidence does not discriminate between explanations (of a discrepancy between sources, a gap in a timeline, a difference in two measures), report the discrepancy and that it is unresolved; do not offer candidate explanations, even labeled. A discrepancy in supporting detail that no required result turns on is reported in one line or omitted.
- **One substantive body.** Organize the body by requested answers. Each result, figure, calculation and conclusion lives together with its interpretation: one or two sentences on what it indicates and what would change that reading. A conclusion spanning topics belongs once where it answers the question; add a separate integration section only if it develops a new inference, not a recap. Do not append a closing summary, takeaways list or repeated recommendation. Refer to an earlier result only when the connection is needed. A consolidated table replaces duplicate per-section figures. Source discussions explain provenance, reliability or why a source meets the request; they refer to the supported result rather than retelling it. If the requested product is itself a source comparison, make that comparison the substantive body.
- **Optional short abstract.** A short answer starts directly with the answer. For a longer report, an abstract of at most three sentences may give the central answer and decisive qualification; do not enumerate every section, obligation or supporting metric. Write it after the body. This limited repetition of the headline answer is intentional. Follow an explicitly requested summary format instead when supplied.
- **Report alternatives, not only the preferred result.** Where a definition or method choice changes a required result materially, show each labeled result and explain which answers the question as asked.
- **Use the field's vocabulary and shape.** Use the precise terms an expert in the domain uses for the quantities and concepts involved. Put parallel figures, periods and options in compact tables with units and definitions visible; keep qualifications beside the result they affect; consolidate repeated caveats and process description. Every figure carries an explicit unit and an unambiguous format (write `1 h 15 min` or `75 min`, not `1:15`). Define a term at first use only when it is below the contract reader's level; do not explain a field's basics to its experts. Body sections follow the question's order; headings are descriptive nouns, never conversational labels.
- **Write in the contract's register.** Formal, impersonal prose throughout the report: third person, no first or second person, no contractions, idioms, colloquial intensifiers or conversational connectives. Calculations are labeled "derived" or "calculated from [source]"; proposals are labeled "Recommendation" or "Proposed"; the researcher's own choices appear as "this review adopts", "the analysis assumes". The disclosures this workflow requires (self-check without independent review, sources not consulted directly, routes that failed, items not extracted) are preserved in full but appear once, in the method-and-limits section, in the same register. Conversational phrasing belongs in progress updates to the user, never in the saved report.
- **Stay honest without hedging.** Preserve adverse evidence and uncertainty where they exist. Precision follows the source; do not invent subgroup results, certainty or settings the evidence does not support. Honest means stating the best-supported figure with its basis and limits, not withholding it.

## Check before delivery

One pass, same context, disclosed as the researcher's own check. It has three parts in this order; do all three.

1. **Coverage against the map.** Walk the answer map, not the report's structure. For every required obligation, locate its line in the report: the value, the interpretation, the recommendation with its fallback, the scenario cell. Add verified missing items from the note; retrieve again only for a genuine evidential gap when acquisition is permitted. For every supporting line, record one of three dispositions: present where a required result or the document type needs it, deliberately left in the note, or not found. This is where most lost points live on the studied tasks: evidence that was found and never reached the report.
2. **Editorial pass against the contract.** This is a reading of meaning, not a scan for words; a vocabulary search does not discharge it. Read the report once as its contract reader and do four things. (a) **Conclusions.** List the report's conclusions in short paraphrase, ignoring wording and figures. Each must have exactly one body home. Consolidate duplicate body conclusions and delete recaps, including source-by-source retellings of the same finding. The optional abstract may briefly repeat the headline answer; it must not become a second account of the body. A restatement in different words is still repetition. (b) **Necessity.** For each paragraph and table, name the obligation it serves. Supporting material that serves none and that the document type does not call for moves to the working note, with its links; a section that only recapitulates others is dissolved into references. (c) **Inference.** For each labeled inference, ask whether the evidence discriminates between readings. If not, rewrite as an unresolved discrepancy with no candidate explanation. (d) **Register and reader fit.** Remove first and second person, contractions, idioms, colloquial intensifiers, conversational headings and hedging adverbs, keeping meaning and labels; delete definitions the reader commands and add those they lack; confirm units and formats; confirm a references section exists when the contract calls for one. **Bound: this pass may not remove a required result, its value, unit, period, definition, calculation or interpretation; the support or labeled alternative of a required result; a qualification attached to a required result; a disclosure; or the source link of any claim that remains. It may remove or relocate supporting detail, repeated conclusions and candidate explanations.** Then walk the required lines of the map a second time, briefly, to confirm each still has its line after editing.
3. **Consequential claims against originals.** For claims whose failure would change a conclusion, a recommendation or a required result, turn the uncertainty into a neutral factual question, reopen the exact passage or data and reconcile the wording with it. Separate compound claims where support differs; verify names, dates, quantities, scope and denominators; confirm each calculation's inputs carry their stated definition. Repair the claim and its dependents locally. Spot-check lower-impact attributions; if a defect appears, widen to that source or error class, not the whole report.

Record in the existing note only consequential corrections with locators, remaining required gaps, and the editorial dispositions as a few lines: which conclusions were consolidated and where their home is, what supporting material was moved to the note, which inferences were rewritten as unresolved. A record that says the pass was done, without these, is not a record of it. No second report, scores, per-sentence ledger, repeated critique cycle or all-clear certificate. A matching link checks structure, not truth.

## Context, tools and stopping

This is a solo workflow. Use the invocation-selected model and reasoning effort throughout. No research delegation, model switching, escalation or extra inference services. If the user explicitly requests an independent review, run it as a disclosed additional step; never recreate a mandatory team.

Use the available search, fetch, file and code-execution tools within the task's access policy; check unfamiliar CLI help first. Parallel tool I/O is fine; research delegation is not. Task-specific extraction or calculation scripts are allowed and must live with the run. A token budget is a ceiling, not a target. After compaction, recover from the working note and reopen decisive originals before writing. Before composing or resuming a draft after compaction, reread the approved question and the original paragraphs in "Write the answer" on body structure, the abstract, inference and register. Use this file, not the handoff's paraphrase; this restores existing instructions rather than adding a review. Check the current clock against any actual host deadline or budget before acting on time pressure. A handoff cannot invent an earlier deadline or a one-batch limit. Reuse these instructions until another compaction; do not reload the whole skill after every tool call.

Stop when every required obligation in the map is filled or explicitly closed as not disclosed or out of reach, every supporting line has a disposition, the consequential claims are checked, and remaining feasible checks are unlikely to change a required result materially. Record the strongest remaining check and the actual reason it was not pursued: a judgment that it would not change the answer, a failed route, or a ceiling that was in fact reached. Do not cite a time or token ceiling that the run did not reach. Elapsed time and source counts certify nothing.

Save the report at the assigned path (`report.md` by default), the working note with the answer map and document contract, and the supporting inventory or provenance the host requires. For project runs, complete the host's source, yield, intervention and lifecycle records after showing the result, per the [workspace integration](references/workspace-integration.md). State in the report's method-and-limits section, in the report's register, that checking was performed by the researcher without independent review. Design rationale and the evidence behind these rules are in [design basis](references/design-basis.md), for maintenance rather than routine loading.
